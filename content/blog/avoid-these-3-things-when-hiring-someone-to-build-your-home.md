Building a new home is one of the biggest money choices most homeowners make. It's also where a few mistakes you could avoid tend to cause the costliest problems. We have done new construction across North Port and Southwest Florida for years. In that time, three issues have come up more than any others.

## Mistake #1: Picking Price Over Track Record

The lowest bid on a new build is rarely the best value. Change orders, delays and rework add up. Some contractors quote far below the rest. Their estimate is often missing part of the job. That missing part shows up later as a cost you didn't plan for. Compare bids line by line, not just by the final number. That makes it much easier to spot this before you sign.

Ask each bidder the same questions. What's included in site prep? How much is set aside for fixtures and finishes? What happens if permits take longer than expected? Then you can compare apples to apples, not just a final number.

### Mistake #2: Skipping the Permit Talk

-   Find out who is pulling the permits, and when
-   Ask if your lot falls under the county or the city
-   Make sure the design meets the flood elevation rules from the start

Skipping this talk is one of the most common reasons new builds stall halfway through. A design can look great on paper. But it can hit a permit snag. That can add weeks or months if no one checked first.

### Mistake #3: Not Getting It All in Writing

Spoken promises don't hold up in a later dispute. That goes for upgrades, timelines or included finishes. Put every change in writing before work goes on. That means changes to materials, the timeline or the scope. You don't do this because you expect a problem. You do it because a written record protects both sides. Memories can differ six months into a job.

## A Fourth Mistake Worth a Mention: Not Planning for Your Site

In Southwest Florida, the soil is sandy and the water table is high. There are also flood elevation rules. Because of this, your site can add cost and time to a new build. That isn't always clear from a standard bid. Soil packing tests, fill needs and drainage plans all depend on your site. Did the contractor walk your lot in detail before the bid? If not, they are more likely to hit a costly surprise once site work begins.

Ask each contractor about other builds nearby with soil or elevation issues like yours. How did they handle them? Ask to see an example. A vague answer here is often a sign of trouble. The site work price may be closer to a guess than a real number.

### What a Full Site Check Looks Like

-   A soil test or soil packing check just for your lot
-   A clear answer on flood elevation rules before the foundation is designed
-   A drainage plan built for Florida's heavy rainy season, not just code minimums

### Putting It All Together

These four mistakes are easy to avoid once you know to look for them. Compare bids on equal footing. Ask direct questions about permits. Put it all in writing. And make sure your contractor has really checked your site. Together, these steps cover most of what makes a new build smooth, not stressful.

## Setting a Real Budget From the Start

There's one more common source of stress in new builds. It's a budget that was never real to begin with. It's tempting to lock onto the lowest number you've heard, even in passing. Then you treat anything above it as going over budget. But the higher number is just what the job really costs.

Set aside a cushion for surprises. It's most often 10 to 15 percent of the total budget. It covers things no one saw coming. It also covers changes you choose to make along the way. It gives you room to adjust. You won't feel like every change blows the budget. Some contractors suggest this buffer from the start. They are generally being more honest with you. Others give an all-in number. It leaves no room for the unexpected.
