Punta Gorda is home to roughly 19,000 residents, with a mix of waterfront homes in Punta Gorda Isles (PGI) and Burnt Store Isles alongside a walkable historic downtown near Fishermen's Village. It's one of the more distinct communities in our service area, and one where remodeling and restoration work each carry their own local considerations. Rebuilt largely after Hurricane Charley in 2004, much of the city's housing stock is newer than what you'll find in some neighboring communities, which shapes what a remodel or addition typically involves here compared to an older inland home.

Remodeling & New Construction in Punta Gorda

Unlike most of Charlotte County, Punta Gorda is an incorporated city, so permitting for projects inside city limits runs through the City of Punta Gorda's building department rather than the county directly. Homes in PGI and Burnt Store Isles, both largely built on filled canal-front lots, also carry flood elevation requirements we check before finalizing scope. The historic downtown district has its own character considerations too - some homes fall within a designated historic area, which can affect what's allowed for exterior changes visible from the street.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Punta Gorda have different permitting than the rest of Charlotte County?

Yes - Punta Gorda is an incorporated city, so permits inside city limits go through the City of Punta Gorda's building department, not Charlotte County directly.

Do PGI and Burnt Store Isles homes need special flood documentation?

Often, yes. Properties on filled canal-front lots in these neighborhoods commonly require flood elevation documentation as part of permitting.

Can you work on homes in the historic downtown district?

Yes, though some properties there fall within a historic review area that can affect exterior changes visible from the street - we check this before finalizing scope.

Is Punta Gorda's housing stock mostly newer construction?

A lot of it, yes - much of the city was rebuilt after Hurricane Charley in 2004, so many homes are newer than in some neighboring communities. We scope each property based on its actual age and condition rather than assuming.

Do you handle commercial projects around Fishermen's Village or downtown Punta Gorda?

Yes, we take on light-commercial remodeling and repair projects in the downtown and waterfront commercial areas as well as residential work.
